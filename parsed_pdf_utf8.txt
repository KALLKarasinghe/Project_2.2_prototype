 1. Introduction  
1.1          Project Title  
 Pharmacy Inventory and Ordering System  
1.2          Project Description  
This project focuses on the design and development of a  web-based B2B pharmacy 
supply management system that connects retail pharmacies with pharmaceutical 
suppliers. The system enables pharmacies to digitally place orders, monitor stock 
levels, and receive timely deliveries. The system  also facilitates  the connection with the 
medical agents for special medicine needs.  
On the supplier side, the system provides tools to manage product listings, update 
inventory quantities, and process incoming orders efficiently. The system also 
integrates automated features such as  inventory tracking, expiry monitoring, and 
notification alerts,  ensuring smooth and reliable pharmaceutical distribution.  
This platform connects medical agents with pharmacies and customers by enabling 
them to obtain specialized and high demand medicines such as chemotherapy drugs, 
which are not accessible through traditional  pharmaceutical distribution channels. It 
also aims to replace traditional manual ordering methods with a more accurate, 
transparent, and scalable digital solution.  
1.3          Background and Motivation   
In the Sri Lankan pharmaceutical supply chain , the process continues to depend on 
traditional and manual communication methods , including phone calls, handwri tten 
documentation, spreadsheets,  and messaging applications. Although these methods 
have been used for many years. They often create significant operational challenges for 
pharmacies and medicine suppliers as manual systems increase the risk of human error, 
reduce efficiency , and negatively impact inventory control and medicine distribution 
processes.  2 
 According  to the World Health Organization, poor inventory management and weak 
communication systems can directly contribute to medicine shortages, treatment 
delays , and the wastage of pharmaceutical products [1].  
The rapid advancement of web technologies platforms provides significant 
opportunities to modify pharmaceutical supply chain operations. Centralized web -
based  systems can improve communication, maintain accurate real-time records, and 
reduce paperwork and human errors.  
The proposed system aims to develop a centralized web-based  platform that connects 
pharmacies, suppliers , and medical agents within a single digital environment, enabling 
pharmacies to search for medicines, place orders online, monitor stock availability , and 
maintain transaction records.  
Another major motivation for this project is the difficulty  of obtaining rare or critical 
medicines that are not readily available through traditional pharmaceutical distribution. 
In Sri Lanka, certain medicines are available only through selected distributors or 
specialized medical agents. Therefore, the proposed sy stem aims to establish a secure 
and centralized connection between customers and verified medical agents to improve 
access to essential medic ines while ensuring reliability . 
1.4     Problem in Brief  
The pharmaceutical supply management system in Sri Lanka faces several operational 
challenges due to manual processes and traditional communication methods. 
Pharmacies usually order medicines through phone calls or messaging applications, 
which may lead to  miscommunication, order errors , and delays in urgent medicine 
orders. In addition, many pharmacies depend on only a limited number of regular 
suppliers. The lack of real-time stock of information makes it difficult to compare prices 
and expiry dates . Rare  drug orders take more time because these medicines are not 
easily available from a single supplier. In most cases , pharmacies need to contact 
several different suppliers to check availability. This process causes delays in medicine 
orders.  
Another major problem is that when medicines are ordered through phone calls, 
pharmacies cannot verify expiry dates. Ordering medicines through phone calls often 3 
 lacks proper documentation, which can lead to overstocking. In advance , which often 
leads to the supply of near expiry  or expired stock. Without proper monitoring and 
communication , pharmacies may stock too many medicines that expire before use , 
leading to waste , while at the same time , they may run out of important medicines when 
they are needed most. Furthermore, rare medicines are often not directly available in 
pharmacies , and expensive medicines must be purchased through medical agents. Many 
patients find it diffic ult to contact these agents and often do not have clear information 
about drug prices.    
1.5          Proposed Solution  
To address the existing challenges in pharmaceutical supply management in Sri Lanka , 
a web-based  application is proposed to create a centralized digital platform connecting 
pharmacies,  customers,  suppliers , and medical agents. This system aims to replace 
manual and fragmented processes by digitizing the entire ordering and inventory 
process. The proposed system ensures better coordination between all stakeholders and 
reduces dependency on traditional communi cation methods such as phone calls and 
informal messaging.  
 
One of the key features of the system is real-time medicine availability checking. This 
allows pharmacies to view updated stock information from suppliers before placing 
orders. This reduces communication errors, speeds up order processing , and improves 
transparency between suppliers and pharmacies. The system also includes real-time 
inventory management for pharmacies , enabling them to continuously monitor stock 
levels and expiry dates. In addition, the platform will provide order tracking and 
complete ord er history management. Another important feature is the integration with 
verified medical agents for handling special cases and rare medicine requirements. This 
ensures that even hard to find or urgent medicines can be sourced efficiently through a 
wider p rofessional network.  
 
Through those digitalized web application features can help reduce overstock and 
medicine waste while improving good communication between suppliers. This 
proactive approach significantly enhances inventory control and minimizes financial 
losses caused by expired or excess medicines.  4 
 1.6   Project Aim and Objective   
1.6.1 Aim:  
To develop an efficient and user-friendly web -based  platform that serves as a channel 
between pharmacies, suppliers, and medical agents, facilitating streamlined 
pharmaceutical ordering and inventory management.  
  
1.6.2 Objectives:  
1) To design a user-friendly  system with secure role-based  access, including 
registration, login, password recovery, and admin controlled license verification 
for pharmacies, companies, medical agents , and customers.  
2) To develop an efficient medicine inventory and order management system that 
enables companies to manage stock, track expiry dates, process orders, and 
allow pharmacies to place, track, and cancel orders.  
3) To implement an integrated payment, billing , and notification system that 
supports multiple payment methods, invoice generation, payment tracking , and 
real time alerts for orders, inventory changes, and system activities.  
4) To provide reporting, analytics, and administrative control features that allow 
system monitoring, sales visualization, commission management, user 
management, and policy enforcement for better decision -making  
 
2.       Methodology   
2.1          Software Development Methodology  
The project will use the Waterfall model as its software development method because 
this model provides a clear and organized development path. The Waterfall model 
works well for this project because it has established requirements for its web-based  
system, which includes product management, order processing and tracking, inventory 
management, and alert functions.  
The project has already determined all system requirements, which will require only 
minor changes throughout the development process. The project team can complete all 5 
 project phases because this method enables better documentation, simpler management, 
and precise task distribution among team members.  
The Waterfall model enables effective project planning and time management, which 
helps to complete the project within the given academic deadline requirements.  
  
2.2 Application of the Waterfall Model in Pharmacy Supply 
Management System  
Phase 1: Requirement Definition  
The first phase of the project started with system requirements identification through 
user needs analysis, which included pharmacy owners, pharmaceutical suppliers, 
medical agents, and system administrators. The team used informal discussions 
together with t heir observations to identify the main problems that occur in the current 
manual system, including ordering delays, stock visibility issues, and expiry date 
tracking difficulties.  
Activities  carried out:  
The team  held informal discussions with pharmacy users, suppliers, and students who 
have knowledge about pharmacy inventory systems in order to identify the issues in 
current pharmacy inventory management processes.  
Through those discussions , the team discovered several key problems , which included:  
the need t o perform the ordering process manually, the inability to provide updates 
about stocks in real time, difficulty in managing expiry dates of medicines, the lack of 
capabilities for generating reports, and challenges in purchasing special medicines.  
Based on the above -identified  problems , the team determined the main  system 
functions required for the proposed system.  The system  will provide  an online ordering 
facility, support inventory  management, notify  users when stock levels reach low 
quantities , send  notifications about product expiry dates , and generate  an analysis of 
sales according to the past records . 
The Waterfall model considers requirement engineering to be an essential stage, which 
enables developers to define all system features before they start building the software 
system.  6

 Phase 3: Implementation  
The system development process follows design specifications during this phase. The 
development team uses module -based development to achieve better project 
organization while simplifying the debugging process.  
Technologies used:  
1) Frontend: React.js  
2) Backend: PHP  
3) Database: MySQL  
Modules implemented:  
1) User authentication module  
2) Product management module  
3) Order processing and tracking module  
4) Inventory management module  
5) Notification system  
Each module is based on the specific user workflows. The system automatically 
updates inventory levels when a pharmacy order is placed while generating alerts 
for low stock situations.  
The Waterfall model ensures that each requirement is translated into a functional 
component during this phase.  
 
Phase 4: Testing  
The system requires testing after implementation to confirm its functional accuracy and 
dependable performance.  
Testing types:  
1) Unit Testing: Testing each module, such as login, order processing, and 
inventory updates  
2) Integration Testing: Ensuring that modules work together (ex, order placement 
updates inventory correctly)  8 
 3) System Testing: Verifying that the entire system meets the defined 
requirements  
Key functionalities tested:  
1) User registration and login  
2) Product management  
3) License and verification management  
4) Order placement and tracking  
5) Inventory updates  
6) Low stock and expiry alerts  
Testing ensures that the system functions operate as expected before the deployment.  
  
Phase 5: Deployment  
The system will enter its operational phase after testing achieves successful results.  
Deployment activities:  
1) Hosting the web application on a server  
2) Configuring the domain and database  
3) Migrating data from development to production  
4) Final system validation process  
The deployment phase ensures that the system is accessible to the users and operates 
smoothly in a real world environment.  
 
Phase 6: Maintenance  
The system requires constant observation with ongoing maintenance from the moment 
of its deployment.  
Maintenance activities:  
1) Fixing bugs and errors  
2) Updating system features  
3) Improving performance  9 
 4) Enhancing security  
User feedback will drive system improvements, which will occur during upcoming 
software updates.  
The Waterfall model defines a distinct maintenance phase that guarantees that systems 
remain usable while they maintain their operational reliability.
